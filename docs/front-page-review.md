  Verdict
  63/100 on front-page copy and hierarchy. The writing is good, but the page tells the
  story in the wrong order. A guest scrolls past architecture, a "day in the life" and "who it's
  for" before learning the price. On a laptop the price first appears about 5,000 px down; on
  a phone it is roughly 9–10 screens down. Meanwhile the booking calendar sits above the
  Alassio section instead of closing the page.

  I reviewed the live site on 28 October 2026 on laptop (1440 × 900) and phone sizes, with
  every line of front-page text read in English. This score uses different criteria from the 27
  October review (copy and hierarchy instead of overall site quality), so the two numbers
  aren't directly comparable.

  What improved since last time: a German version, a WhatsApp "Ask a question" button,
   stay@dolceazzurra.com instead of Gmail, a real evening photo (the lit pool), an Alassio
  photo in the teaser, reviews shortened to two plus "More reviews", the price lowered to
  "from €700", a "Your hosts" block on The Villa page, a bigger gallery (36 photos), and a
  rewritten Alassio heading.

  Top fixes:

   1. Reorder the front page: essentials first (what, where, how many, how much), then
      trust (reviews, architect, hosts), and the booking tool last.
   2. Put the price and capacity in the hero: "Sleeps 8 in 4 suites · from €700 a night".
   3. Drop "Life as it should be" from the footer. You're right that it is out of tone; the site's
      strength is understatement.
   4. Cut or move two lyrical sections ("A day here", "Who it's for") to The Villa page,
      keeping one line each on the front page.
   5. Fix the desktop hero video: it opens on the house front with neighbouring houses and
      no sea. Open on the view.
   6. Drop "everything included": cleaning is a separate €440 fee on every stay, so show it
      next to the price instead of claiming everything is included.

       Essentials first lets a guest qualify themselves in seconds: right size, right area, right
       budget. Those who don't fit leave early; those who do keep reading with intent.
       Trust second answers the next question, "Is it really as good as it looks?": reviews from
       real, named guests, the architect and award, and who the hosts are.
       Booking last catches the guest at the moment of highest intent, with every remaining
       doubt (included, minimum stay, cancellation) answered right next to the calendar.
       The "Check dates" button in the header and the sticky phone bar stay, so ready guests
       can jump straight down at any time.

  What leaves the front page: "A day here" and "Who it's for" move to The Villa page, where
  a guest who wants more depth will read them. On the front page they become a single line
  each inside "The villa in 3 points".
